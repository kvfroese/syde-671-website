# Implementation Details

## Linear Least Squares

We start by stating the basic epipolar constraint, where $\mathbf{x}_2$ and $\mathbf{x}_1$ are homogeneous coordinates of corresponding points from Image 1 and Image 2.

$$
\begin{align*}
\mathbf{x}_2^\top F \mathbf{x}_1&= 0 \\
\begin{bmatrix}
u_2 & v_2 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
f_{11} & f_{12} & f_{13} \\
f_{21} & f_{22} & f_{23} \\
f_{31} & f_{32} & f_{33}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
u_1 \\ v_1 \\ 1
\end{bmatrix}
&= 0
\end{align*}
$$

To get a more useful form, we can expand out the matrices by multiplying and doing some matrix manipulation.

$$
\begin{align*}
\begin{bmatrix}
u_2 & v_2 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
u_1 f_{11} + v_1 f_{12} + f_{13} \\
u_1 f_{21} + v_1 f_{22} + f_{23} \\
u_1 f_{31} + v_1 f_{32} + f_{33}
\end{bmatrix}
&= 0 \\
\begin{bmatrix}
(u_2 u_1 f_{11} + u_2 v_1 f_{12} + u_2 f_{13}) + (v_2 u_1 f_{21} + v_2 v_1 f_{22} + v_2 f_{23}) + (u_1 f_{31} + v_1 f_{32} + f_{33}) 
\end{bmatrix}
&= 0 \\
\implies
\begin{bmatrix}
u_2 u_1 & u_2v_1 & u_2 & v_2 u_1 & v_2 v_1 & v_2 & u_1 & v_1 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
f_{11} \\ f_{12} \\ f_{13} \\ f_{21} \\ f_{22} \\ f_{23} \\ f_{31} \\ f_{32} \\ f_{33}
\end{bmatrix}
&= 0
\end{align*}
$$
We end up with a very useful relationship, allowing us to perform a first singular value decomposition (SVD) to get a least squares solution.  We only care about the right singular vectors $V^\top$ here and pick the smallest value ($\sigma_3$) to be set to 0 to impose the rank-2 constraint and ensure epipolar geometry.

We then perform another SVD to find an optimal solution post-constraint, minimizing the Frobenius norm. We obtain the left singular vectors $U'$, the diagonal singular values $\Sigma'$, and the right singular vectors ${V^\top}'$.

We impose a rank constraint on $\Sigma_3=0$. Then we may multiply our components to obtain the fundamental matrix by

$$
F = U'\Sigma'V^\top
$$

## Epipolar Distance


The first step of calculating epipolar distance requires calculating a line with the fundamental matrix as

$$
l_2=p_i F^\top
$$
From $l_2$ we can obtain $a,b,c$ by extracting the columns representing the separate coefficients.

We calculate the epipolar distance using perpendicular distance, with the formula

$$
d_\perp =\frac{\abs{ au + bv + c }}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}
$$

This one-way distance works by applying the first points onto the second image as epipolar lines. A symmetric distance can be calculated by doing the same operation twice, but using the second points and first image, as well as performing a transpose. From here the distances could be added, averaged, average squared, etc. as could be used in further techniques.
